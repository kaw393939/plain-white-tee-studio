# Three possible meanings

The shirt stays the same. These territories change the situation, promise, and presentation around it. They are editorial hypotheses for review, not validated audiences or selected brands. All names are provisional and uncleared.

## 01 — Dayform

### An everyday system

**Audience hypothesis:** someone moving between study, work, and errands who wants less effort assembling an everyday outfit.

**Desired meaning:** a dependable starting point for the day. “Dependable” here describes the proposed role in a wardrobe, not a tested durability or fit claim.

| Brief element | Proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Working promise | Start with one simple piece. Make the day your own. |
| Primary archetype | Everyperson: approachable and practical |
| Voice | Clear, calm, useful |
| Helpful page content | A few outfit combinations for specific everyday situations, plus plainly stated known product information |
| Secondary need | Someone who enjoys expressive clothing and wants a quiet base beneath it |
| First question to test | Does organizing the page around daily situations make choosing an outfit feel easier? |

**Visual possibilities:** a modernist treatment could use an explicit grid and numbered outfit sequences. A postmodernist treatment could juxtapose fragments of a day while retaining the same content and action.

**Risk:** this territory can become interchangeable “essentials” language. It needs useful, specific guidance to earn attention. It should not imply that the product has proven performance advantages.

## 02 — Common Ground

### A shared starting point for creative people

**Audience hypothesis:** someone heading to a campus creative session, studio, or informal collaboration who wants room for personal expression without wearing a loud brand.

**Desired meaning:** an open canvas that leaves the person and their work in the foreground.

| Brief element | Proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Working promise | A common starting point. Your own way forward. |
| Primary archetype | Creator: curious and expressive |
| Voice | Open, playful, encouraging |
| Helpful page content | Original styling or composition prompts that a visitor can use freely |
| Secondary need | Someone who likes the creative context but prefers a restrained outfit |
| First question to test | Does the creative framing feel inviting, or does it make the product seem like a club with entry rules? |

**Visual possibilities:** a modernist treatment could use modular editorial layouts and a deliberate image sequence. A postmodernist treatment could mix quoted visual languages, annotations, and layered framing without changing the copy or product.

**Risk:** community is easy to imply and difficult to substantiate. Publish actual contributions only with permission. Avoid invented members, collaborations, reviews, or belonging claims. Creative activities need to work even before a real community exists.

## 03 — First Layer

### A small ritual for a new beginning

**Audience hypothesis:** a student or early-career person preparing for a first day, a new group, or an informal event who wants to feel put together while remaining themselves.

**Desired meaning:** a simple starting point when the occasion feels unfamiliar. This is a proposed emotional role, not a guarantee of confidence or a claim that a T-shirt suits every dress code.

| Brief element | Proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Working promise | Start with a blank. Show up as yourself. |
| Primary archetype | Everyperson: warm and reassuring |
| Voice | Direct, warm, understated |
| Helpful page content | A short occasion-aware outfit guide with an honest reminder to check the setting’s dress expectations |
| Secondary need | Someone already comfortable with the occasion who wants a simple base for their existing style |
| First question to test | Does this framing address a real preparation concern, or does it make a basic shirt feel unnecessarily serious? |

**Visual possibilities:** a modernist treatment could create a clear preparation sequence and generous reading space. A postmodernist treatment could use the visual language of notes, invitations, or first-day ephemera while preserving the same message and action.

**Risk:** avoid exploiting insecurity or suggesting that buying a shirt is necessary to belong. Offer useful guidance freely; let the visitor decide whether the product fits their situation.

## Which deserves the first prototype?

**First Layer is the provisional recommendation.** It offers a concrete moment to design around and a useful first content feature without relying on unverified product superiority. That makes it practical to test within the [project brief](/project/brief). This is an editorial judgment about prototype scope, not evidence of an unoccupied market or proven demand.

Dayform is a useful comparison if people respond more strongly to reducing everyday effort. Common Ground deserves further work if actual creative participation becomes available. These can remain alternatives; combining all three would blur the first page’s purpose.

See the [recommendation](/strategy/recommendation) for the proposed positioning and the [roadmap](/project/roadmap) for the decisions and evidence still needed.
