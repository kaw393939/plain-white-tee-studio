Product and certification statements are attributed to their brands; we did not audit supply chains, test garments or purchase products. Prices, promotions, variants and availability may change.

[Product page · style 2135P6](https://www.hanes.com/products/hanes-men-s-undershirt-pack-in-white-v-neck-moisture-wicking-100-cotton-with-odor-control-6-pack/2135p6)

- **Observed:** US$20 for six white undershirts. The brand lists 100% preshrunk cotton, tagless construction, a longer length for tucking and moisture/odor features.

We retain the style number and neutral undershirt label instead of resolving that conflict by assumption. No selected-size availability, shipping or tax verified.

## How to use this evidence

The explorer groups examples into **Value, Premium, Responsibility and Culture** for discussion. These are editorial labels, not objective scores or mutually exclusive market segments. A brand can communicate several promises at once.

The sample does not measure market share, demand, price elasticity, customer preference or a gap in the market. It is deliberately useful for a fixed-product branding exercise: compare **audience, promise, proof and visual communication**, then test whether intended wearers understand our own concepts.

Refresh the product page, date, currency, pack basis, selected color and exact price whenever updating the landscape. Record uncertainty explicitly. Keep source clones, raw captures and local reference material in the ignored `references/` folder; the published site retains only the analysis and source links.
