# The project brief

Give one plain white T-shirt a meaning that a specific person can recognize. The immediate task is to compare opportunities and decide what deserves a prototype.

**Status:** research and proposals for review. No final audience, brand name, promise, or visual direction has been selected.

## What the source project asks us to do

The [IS117 Brand Studio README](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/README.md) starts with a deliberately neutral storefront and a working size selector and temporary demo bag. Its central exercise is to express audience, character, voice, and visual decisions through that same product. The [source specification](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/SPEC.md) leaves the brand choices open.

This review site is an independent planning companion. Its proposals do not complete the template’s student decision record or establish customer research.

| Keep fixed | Explore deliberately |
| --- | --- |
| The same plain white T-shirt | A concrete wearing situation and the meaning someone seeks |
| The same known product facts | Brand name, promise, character, and voice |
| The same content across the two visual previews | Typography, hierarchy, composition, and image treatment |
| Honest description of a classroom demo | Useful ways to help a visitor choose voluntarily |

## The strategic question

What would make someone understand this shirt’s role in their life before asking them to believe it is technically superior?

The [competitive landscape](/research/landscape) compares how existing brands explain value. It is a set of examples, not a market-size estimate. The [idea territories](/strategy/ideas) turn those observations into three hypotheses. The [recommendation](/strategy/recommendation) identifies a direction worth testing first.

## A small first deliverable

Build one coherent product page and its live style guide after a direction is chosen. Include an original SVG wordmark, a useful product area, the existing size and demo-bag interaction, and two honest persuasion applications. Share the actual design tokens and assets between the page and guide.

Before building the full page, compare modernist and postmodernist hero treatments using **the same shirt, headline, supporting copy, product facts, and action**. Each treatment needs a specific, inspected design reference, a short explanation of its choices, an intended audience effect, and a risk. These are future prototypes; this review site does not claim that comparison has been completed.

## Claims we can support

At this stage, the project supplies a white T-shirt and a demo interaction. Fabric composition, manufacturing location, fit performance, sustainability, durability, shipping terms, and guarantees need real evidence before appearing as facts. A competitor’s claim is not evidence about our product.

Any proposed price is a fictional scenario until a product and commercial basis exist. Names on this site are working labels and have not received trademark or domain clearance. No interviews, purchases, conversion results, or learner observations are claimed.

## What a useful review should decide

1. Which concrete wearing situation should lead the first prototype?
2. What one-sentence promise can we make truthfully with the available evidence?
3. Which contrasting need should the same page also accommodate?
4. What would we need to observe to revise or keep the direction?

Use the [roadmap](/project/roadmap) to move from those choices to a reviewable slice.
